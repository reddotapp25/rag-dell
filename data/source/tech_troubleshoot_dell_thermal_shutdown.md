# How to Stop Computer and Laptop Overheating and Shutdown Issues

Source: https://www.dell.com/support/kbdoc/en-us/000130867/how-to-troubleshoot-a-overheating-shutdown-or-thermal-issue-on-a-dell-pc
Dell KB article: 000130867
Company: Dell

## Summary

Is your personal computer overheating, or is your laptop running hot and
shutting down? This guide covers the causes of laptop overheating, how to
prevent an overheating laptop, and steps to fix a computer that overheated
and shut down.

Your Dell computer can overheat because of insufficient airflow due to
dust or debris blocking the air vents, exhaust ports, or a clogged fan.
An overheating computer can intermittently restart, shut down, or
experience performance issues.

## Symptoms of thermal issues

When the computer overheats, you may experience:

- Intermittent shutdowns or unexpected restarts.
- Performance slowdowns or sudden throttling under load.
- Loud fan noise or the fan running at maximum speed constantly.
- Error messages such as:
  - "Alert! Air Temperature Sensor Not Detected."
  - "Fan, heat sink, or air temperature-related error message."

The most common reason for an overheating computer is dust buildup on the
fans or air vents. Reducing the heat inside the computer improves system
performance and prevents damage to internal components.

## Troubleshooting steps (in sequence)

### 1. Run the automated tools in SupportAssist

Dell Technologies recommends ensuring that the device drivers and BIOS are
up to date. Use Dell SupportAssist to automatically scan and update.

1. Select SupportAssist in the Start menu.
2. Run the "Update Software" scan.
3. Apply any available BIOS and driver updates.
4. Restart if prompted.

If the issue persists, go to the next step.

### 2. Inspect and clean the air vents and fans

Dust in the computer restricts airflow. This can cause the processor,
memory, and other components to overheat.

1. Turn the computer off and unplug the AC adapter.
2. Use canned compressed air to blow dust from the vents.
3. Hold the can upright and use short bursts; do not tilt the can.
4. Avoid using your mouth or a vacuum (static discharge risk).
5. Reconnect power and turn the computer on.

If the issue persists, go to the next step.

### 3. Run the hardware diagnostics

Running a hardware diagnostic test can help you identify hardware-related
problems and provides troubleshooting steps.

1. Restart the computer.
2. At the Dell logo, press F12 several times to enter the One-Time Boot
   Menu.
3. Select Diagnostics and press Enter.
4. Follow the on-screen prompts.
5. If the tests pass, go to the next step.
6. If the tests fail, write down the error code and validation code and
   contact Dell Technical Support.

### 4. Use Dell Power Manager or Dell Optimizer to manage thermal performance

Applicable for select Dell laptops. Dell Power Manager / Dell Optimizer
allow you to manage processor and cooling-fan settings. You can manage
performance, choose a thermal profile such as Optimized or Cooler, and
tune battery-charging policies.

- On older Inspiron, Vostro, and XPS systems, use Dell Power Manager.
- On current systems, use Dell Optimizer or MyDell (Dell Power Manager is
  discontinued and integrated into these).
- Set the thermal profile to "Optimized" or "Cool" for heat-sensitive
  workloads.

If the issue persists, go to the next step.

### 5. Tips to improve efficiency and minimize overheating

- Keep the laptop on a flat, hard surface. Avoid soft surfaces such as
  beds, blankets, or laps, which block vents.
- Use a laptop cooling pad for demanding workloads.
- Keep the ambient room temperature between 0 °C and 35 °C (32 °F–95 °F)
  with normal humidity.
- Close unnecessary applications and background processes.
- Avoid direct sunlight or placing the laptop near heat sources.
- Keep BIOS and drivers current (see step 1).

### 6. Factory reset, restore, or reinstall Windows (last resort)

Dell computers are built with a small amount of hard-disk space reserved
for reinstalling the operating system. If thermal issues coincide with OS
instability, see the Dell KB article "Factory Reset, Restore, or Reinstall
Microsoft Windows on a Dell computer."

If the issue persists after all these steps, contact Dell Technical
Support.
